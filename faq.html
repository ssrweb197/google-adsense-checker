AdSense Policy FAQ

Authoritative answers to the most common webmaster compliance questions.

1. How long does the Google AdSense approval review take? ▼
Reviews generally take between 24 hours to 2 weeks. In rare cases involving complex domain structures, it can take up to 4 weeks.
2. How many articles do I need before applying? ▼
We recommend publishing at least 20 to 30 well-researched, original posts (minimum 1,000 words each) categorized neatly in navigation menus.
3. What does "Low Value Content" mean? ▼
This rejection error occurs when Google's automated systems detect thin content, duplicated text from other sites, auto-generated content without unique added insight, or pages lacking organic user traffic.
4. Are Privacy Policy and Legal pages mandatory? ▼
Yes. Google requires clear disclosures regarding cookie usage and third-party ad vendors. You must have accessible Privacy Policy, Terms & Conditions, and Contact Us pages.
5. Can I get approved on a free domain subdomain (e.g., Blogspot)? ▼
Yes, Google AdSense approves official Blogspot subdomains. However, custom top-level domains (.com, .org, .net) yield much higher approval rates across independent platforms.
6. Is an SSL Certificate (HTTPS) required? ▼
Yes. Google prioritizes user security as a primary page experience signal. Unencrypted HTTP domains face immediate technical flags.
7. Can I apply for AdSense with AI-generated content? ▼
While AI tools can assist writing, publishing unedited raw AI articles often triggers "Low Value Content" or programmatic spam filters. Articles must include original value, human editing, and unique insights.
8. What is the minimum age requirement to apply for AdSense? ▼
Applicants must be at least 18 years old. If you are under 18, a parent or legal guardian must apply using their own verified Google Account.
9. Can I place AdSense ads alongside other ad networks? ▼
Yes, provided the alternative ad network adheres to Google's content policies and the overall page layout isn't overcrowded with ad units.
10. Why was my site rejected for "Site Navigation" issues? ▼
This occurs when menus contain broken links, point to empty categories, lack intuitive structure, or use deceptive UI element placements.
11. Does my website need to be a specific age before applying? ▼
In certain regions (such as India and China), Google historically preferred domains to be at least 6 months old. Generally, quality content and organic traffic matter more than raw domain age.
12. Does website speed affect AdSense approval? ▼
Indirectly, yes. Mobile usability and Core Web Vitals contribute to page experience scoring during automated compliance evaluations.
13. What languages does Google AdSense support? ▼
AdSense supports over 50 major languages. Ensure your primary publishing language is supported in Google's official AdSense language roster.
14. Can I monetization a single page website? ▼
It is exceedingly difficult. Google favors multi-page websites featuring organized navigation structures and continuous content publishing.
15. How much organic traffic do I need for approval? ▼
There is no official traffic threshold, but having consistent organic search traffic demonstrates real user engagement to Google's approval reviewers.
16. What content topics are prohibited by AdSense policies? ▼
Adult content, illegal downloads/piracy, hate speech, dangerous products, copyrighted media, and deceptive financial schemes are strictly banned.
17. How do I fix a "Site Down or Unavailable" rejection error? ▼
Verify your robots.txt file isn't blocking Googlebot, remove aggressive firewall challenges (like Cloudflare Under Attack Mode), and ensure valid DNS records.
18. What is an ads.txt file and do I need it? ▼
An ads.txt (Authorized Digital Sellers) file protects your ad inventory from fraud. AdSense requires adding a verified ads.txt line to your site root after approval.
19. Can I re-apply if my application is rejected? ▼
Yes, there is no limit on re-applications. Address every highlighted issue in your rejection report before re-submitting.
20. What is Google E-E-A-T and why does it matter for monetization? ▼
E-E-A-T stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. Google uses these quality signals to ensure sites showing ads deliver reliable value.
